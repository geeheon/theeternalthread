TWO HUSBANDS

The source changes everything.

ROMANS 7:1–6 · AFTER WATCHMAN NEE
"For the woman who has a husband is bound by the law to her husband as long as he lives. But if the husband dies, she is released from the law of her husband."
ROMANS 7:2
PAUL'S ILLUSTRATION
Before Paul reaches the agony of Romans 7:18 — "to will is present with me, but how to perform what is good I do not find" — he gives an illustration. A woman married to two husbands, one after the other. Same standard. Different source. The illustration explains why the performing always failed — and why it doesn't have to.
THE FIRST MARRIAGE
Bound to the law — Romans 7:1–3
I
THE FIRST HUSBAND
The law — Romans 7:1–3
Tap to expand

A woman meets a man. She falls in love. They marry. They go on honeymoon.

They return home, and the husband tells her he would like breakfast every morning at 4am. Not just any breakfast — a very specific breakfast. Two eggs, sunny-side up, yolks still runny but whites fully set. Three rashers of bacon — crispy but not burnt, no chewy fat. Sourdough toast, medium-brown, butter applied while still hot so it melts evenly. Sliced avocado, fanned on the plate, with a pinch of flaky salt and cracked pepper. Sautéed mushrooms — cremini, not button — cooked in butter until golden, no sogginess. A small bowl of fresh fruit — seasonal, no melon. Freshly squeezed orange juice, no pulp. Coffee at exactly 82 degrees, long black, served in the ceramic mug, not the glass one. Every morning. Without fail. At 4am.

The requirement is not unreasonable in itself. Each item is good. The husband is upright, blameless even. There is nothing wrong with what he asks. It is good.

At first she does it. She sets the alarm. She rises. She cooks. But after a few mornings, she misses the alarm. The bacon is overdone. The eggs break. The avocado is bruised. She forgets the mushrooms. The coffee is too hot. She gets sloppy, exhausted, resentful. The list never shrinks. The standard never bends.

He doesn't help. He can't help. That's not how law works. Law states the requirement. It doesn't cook the breakfast. It tells you what is right — and watches while you fail to produce it.

The requirement was good.
The source was her.
She could not sustain it.

Joined to Christ — Romans 7:4–6
II
THE SECOND HUSBAND
Christ — Romans 7:4–6
Tap to expand

She goes to heaven. She meets another man — equally upright, equally blameless. They fall in love. They marry. They go on honeymoon.

They return home, and this husband tells her exactly the same thing. The identical requirement. Breakfast at 4am. Two eggs, sunny-side up. Three rashers of bacon, crispy. Sourdough toast, medium-brown. Fanned avocado with flaky salt. Sautéed cremini mushrooms in butter. Fresh seasonal fruit. Freshly squeezed orange juice, no pulp. Long black at 82 degrees in the ceramic mug. Every morning. Every detail the same.

Her heart sinks. She remembers the first marriage. The exhaustion. The failure. The guilt of never meeting the standard. She sets the alarm for 4am.

The alarm goes off. She drags herself to the kitchen — and it's already made.

The eggs are perfect. The bacon is crispy. The avocado is fanned. The mushrooms are golden. The juice is fresh. The coffee is exactly right — in the ceramic mug. The second husband is standing in the kitchen. He has done it himself. Not because she failed — but because this is who he is. He doesn't just state the requirement. He fulfils it.

The next morning, the same thing. And the next. She never has to perform what she could never sustain. The standard hasn't changed. The source has.

The requirement is the same.
The source is him.
Grace doesn't lower the standard — it meets it.
"Therefore, my brethren, you also have become dead to the law through the body of Christ, that you may be married to another — to Him who was raised from the dead, that we should bear fruit to God."
ROMANS 7:4
THE POINT
The problem with the first husband was never the breakfast. The law is holy, just, and good (Rom 7:12). The problem was the source — it demanded from her what she could never sustain. The second husband has the same standard but a different nature. He doesn't demand and watch. He provides what he requires. This is the difference between law and grace. Grace doesn't cancel the standard. It fulfils it from a different source. Christ in you — meeting the righteous requirement of the law (Rom 8:4) — not by your effort but by His life.

WHAT PAUL IS SHOWING
The logic of Romans 7:1–6
"But now we have been delivered from the law, having died to what we were held by, so that we should serve in the newness of the Spirit and not in the oldness of the letter."
ROMANS 7:6
THE RELEASE
The woman didn't try harder under the first husband until she finally succeeded. She died — and in dying, was released. That death was not her own achievement. It was Christ's death, counted as hers (Rom 6:6). And in that release, she was joined to another — to Him who rose from the dead. The standard didn't change. The source did. Everything did.

The law could only demand.
Grace does what law never could —
it meets its own requirement
from a different source.
